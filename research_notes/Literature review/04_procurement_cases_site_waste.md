## D52 Reuse toolkit: procurement strategies

**Identity.**
- FCRBE (Interreg NWE 739), deliverable WP T3 D1.2, February 2022, 172 pages (L1-18, L103-105).
- Authors: Rotor (Geerts, Ghyoot, Naval) and Bellastock (Topalov), with Brussels Environment, the Construction Confederation, BBRI and CSTB (L114-132). A public-procurement lawyer reviewed a draft (L134-137).
- Authority: high for procurement mechanics. Medium when applied to the UK: it predates the Procurement Act 2023 and assumes the French and Belgian ten-year insurance regime.

**Summary.**
- The guide tells owners how to turn a wish to reuse materials into contract terms.
  - Part 1: defining a reuse objective.
  - Part 2: procurement routes.
  - Part 3: 18 factsheets.
- Central ideas:
  - reuse needs flexibility (variants, substitution, price ranges, tolerances) with new materials as the fallback;
  - **inflow** (reclaimed materials brought in) and **outflow** (materials reclaimed from the existing building) are separate objectives;
  - prior market consultation is "nearly indispensable".
- Cost position: reclaimed prices are "comparable to, and even competitive with" new; aim for cost neutrality rather than savings.

**Objective** (L353-799):
- Two axes, qualitative or quantitative and open or specific, with template phrases. Example: "integrate at least X% of this reclaimed material for this part" (L529-542).
- Scan actors, sources and policy before setting ambition (L353-471).
- The objective sits in the environmental performance section of the contract and is cross-referenced (L561-567).
- Priority order: conservation in place, then on-site reuse, then local reclaimed supply (L594-675).
- Ambition levels: minimum (specifications), intermediate (dealer visits plus a protocol to validate purchases), high (a batch built up over time) (L759-778).
- Reuse assistance by a consultant, either hired by the client or in the bidder's team (L780-799).

**Routes** (L884-995):
- A, progressive: two contracts; major reuse options fixed by the end of design.
- B, design and build: a quantitative target that becomes contractual on award.
- Add-ons:
  - **the client buys batches through a separate supply contract** and the contractor installs only (examples at L1315-1367);
  - a framework agreement;
  - a **"reuse lot"** that finds, collects, prepares, stores, guards and documents reclaimed materials for the other trades. At Colombelles, 3% of a €4.9m budget went through it (L1500-1524).

**Sources** (factsheet 3):
- dealers;
- the original building, with removal and reinstallation decoupled (L1883-1914);
- coinciding sites revealed by digital platforms (L1961-1979);
- internal stocks, where temporary storage "considerably widens the window of opportunity" (L2113-2131).

**Model clauses (verbatim in the source).**
- **Review clause** for when reused supplies cannot be obtained (L3456-3553). It applies if the price falls within the client's estimated range. The contractor proves unavailability with several quotes and the reuse adviser's view; the client then chooses new at an agreed price or the reused supplies available. Allowed below 15% of contract value (L3428-3432).
- **Insurance clause:** the contractor obtains the insurer's conditions and may state any extra premium (L3739-3752).
- **Execution clause:** an identity sheet per batch, and quantity counted after dismantling (100 m2 taken down with 60 m2 reclaimed counts as 60) (L5131-5174).
- **Origin:** invoice, storage sheet or proof of direct origin; at minimum a sworn statement (L5186-5204, L5454-5465).
- **Off-site acquisition:** an obligation of means, with written justification before switching to new (L5475-5502).
- **Dismantling clauses:**
  - careful dismantling as an obligation of means;
  - storage and packaging conditions;
  - a dismantling trial to set the loss rate;
  - presumed quantities, and "only the surfaces actually recovered will be paid";
  - a surplus for repairs;
  - do not force the dismantler to reinstall (L4046-4146).

**Award weightings.**
- Housing design: sustainability 25%, including "exemplary materials management" (L4789-4804).
- Urban works: price 40% and technical 60%, of which 10% for the reuse approach, split 5 points storage method and 5 points supply method (L4817-4834).
- Renovation: price 80% and method 20%, including storage of reuse supplies (L4836-4853).
- A Brussels design and build scored minimum mass targets for retention, outgoing reuse, outgoing recycling, inbound reuse and recycled content (L1175-1211).

**Specification wording** (L3808-4355):
- widen tolerances and specify intervals;
- accept patina and shade;
- use laying methods that absorb variation;
- regulatory requirements are "non-negotiable";
- for a known batch, list the operations;
- for an unknown batch, specify performance with thresholds;
- a porphyry sett of fixed 13 cm could have been "between 10 and 20 cm";
- a 2,600 m2 barnwood cladding specification;
- reserve CE requirements for new materials (L5383-5387);
- planning applications use dimension ranges with a new fallback (L5428-5452).

**Liability:** early involvement of the insurer and control office; client ownership; a contractor commitment in design and build; a quality plan dividing duties (L3661-3737, L4959-4991).

**Timing:**
- ambitions at programming;
- audit before demolition;
- consultation before the tender;
- options fixed by end of design;
- structural batches bought during design;
- monitoring at each stage;
- a report at the end.

**Case numbers.**
- Reclaimed granite roadworks averaged 57% dearer than new, about €80,000 extra, "barely 0.8% of the total budget" (L3239-3257).
- On-site brick reuse, per m3: careful removal €190, cleaning and sorting €80, packaging €17 (L3259-3284).
- SEGRO, Slough, 2013: a 3,320 m2 building relocated. 70% reused by mass, 40% less CO2 and 25% lower cost, £2.5m over 56 weeks. Reused: steel frame, precast planks, raised floor, curtain walling and lift. Bricks could not be reclaimed; as-built drawings were inaccurate (L2024-2108).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Specification structure and clauses.
- The price guide can feed the review clause's estimated price range and a schedule of reclaimed and new unit prices.
- Package costs should add dismantling, cleaning and packaging lines and a client sum for tests and losses.
- Who pays for testing depends on the route:
  - a client provisional sum in traditional contracts;
  - the contractor in design and build;
  - client feasibility tests before the tender.
- The reuse lot task list is close to Tallyard's agency and brokerage services.
- "Needs known in advance" supports forward sale.
- Outflow and inflow targets.
- RIBA-stage gates.

**Contradictions.**
- Low fixed reuse ratios (0.1 to 0.4) clash with "comparable to new" and granite at 1.57 times new.
- Price depends on preparation state, not only condition.
- Handling rates leave out the costliest step, preparation.
- The 60-day timeline test assumes overlap is needed, where storage and early purchase are the normal bridge.
- Naming one listing in a public tender can restrict competition; use performance specifications or client purchase with free issue.

**Gaps.** No UK procurement law, UK insurance model, prices for steel, curtain wall or precast, storage costs or testing costs.

**Feature ideas.**
- Clause and award-criteria generator.
- Price-range export.
- Batch identity sheet.
- Route selector with responsibility matrix.
- Market consultation annex built from listings.
- Factsheet 18 report.
- Monitoring table by RIBA stage.

**Categories.** 10, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18.

## D39 Ex-post analysis of 32 works: results and discussions

**Identity.** FCRBE deliverable WP T4.2.2, September 2023, by the same team. Authority high for method, medium for generalising (small pioneering sample, L2209-2244).

**Method.**
- Inflow reuse rates by mass per layer: structure, skin, space plan, services and outdoor layers. Furniture is excluded (L296-347).
- Each bill-of-quantities line is tagged new or reused, assigned to a layer and converted to mass, using technical sheets first, then ICE or KBOB, then stated assumptions (L349-455).
- Avoided carbon is A1-A3 of the product family, with biogenic carbon removed at 1.637 kgCO2 per kg of wood (L2043-2198).

**Indicative targets by layer, by mass** (L1830-1851):

| Layer | Proposed target |
|---|---|
| Structure | 1 to 5% |
| Skin | 5 to 15% |
| Space plan | 10 to 25% |
| Outdoor surfaces | 30 to 50% |

Services get no target; count pieces instead (L1980-1984).

**Category results.**
- Tertiary new-build structure averaged 4.93% (range 1.75 to 9.58%), with 30 to 160 t reused: "a useful indication of a plausible target" (L1451-1474).
- Tertiary skin averaged 14.49% (L1476-1499).
- Housing skin averaged 23%, where facing brick often exceeds 20% (L1301-1330).
- It is "premature to require the systematic reuse of structural elements" (L1891-1895).
- Visible services are reused; hidden pipes almost never (L1958-1962).

**Conversion defaults.**
- Use conservative values (L2436-2443).
- Mortar joint 12 mm. Mortar is 20 to 30% of a brick wall's surface, so an all-reclaimed brick wall is never 100% reused (L2445-2467).
- Pavers 80/20 with a 3 cm bed (L2469-2488).
- Glazing 20 kg/m2 double, 35 kg/m2 security (L2490-2517).
- Services 13, 4 and 3 kg/m2 for HVAC, electrical and sanitary (L2519-2542).
- Raised floor belongs to space plan; facades and brick cladding to skin (L2571-2701).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- A per-layer reuse rate by mass on the consultant dashboard, with these bands.
- Conversion defaults from units to mass.
- A stated carbon boundary.
- A warning that structure targets above about 5% by mass are rarely met.
- Run the calculation from the bill of quantities at RIBA stage 4.

**Contradictions.**
- Tallyard's A1-A4 boundary goes beyond FCRBE's A1-A3. If transport is credited, the reclaimed item's own haulage must be charged.
- Value and mass diverge: Dilbeek was 25% by mass and 50% by cost.

**Gaps.** The companion reports on setting and reporting rates and on live tests are cited but absent.

**Categories.** 9, 8, 15, 17, 13.

## D32 Site waste management plan, Museum Street

**Identity.** A demolition contractor's plan for soft strip and demolition at a Camden site, about 2023. Authority medium (a real project document).

**Content.**
- The waste hierarchy and duty of care (Environmental Protection Act 1990); hazardous waste consignment notes; WM3 classification.
- **Targets:**
  - 98% of non-hazardous demolition waste reused, recycled or recovered, against the 95% policy;
  - excavation 95% to beneficial use;
  - construction 95%;
  - **recycled content 23.7% by value against the 20% policy aim**.
- BREEAM benchmarks: no more than 7.5 m3 and 6.5 t per 100 m2 GIA. Diversion targets:

  | Waste | By volume | By mass |
  |---|---|---|
  | Non-demolition | 70% | 80% |
  | Demolition | 80% | 90% |

- Routes per stream: timber to a community wood recycler; glass recycled, with intact windows to a reuse broker; brick recycled pending a reuse study; plasterboard 95% recycled; fit-out items to a reuse broker.
- A register of waste carriers and permits with EWC codes and distance from site.

**Informs Tallyard.**
- The waste bill import should carry carrier licence, facility and distance.
- Diversion should be reported by volume and by mass.
- Waste per 100 m2 benchmarks.
- Example of a project beating the 20% aim by value.
- The demolition contractor as a role.

**Categories.** 7, 2.

## D42 Construction Waste Indicative Cost (CWIC) calculator, user guide

**Identity.** Zero Waste Scotland; calculator developed by Glasgow Caledonian University; costs as at March 2022. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- Setup: construction type, phase, weather, distances to the skip, skip size (L35-178).
- Each waste row: type with an EWC code (chapter 17), the reason it is in the skip (22 reasons, from design error to over-ordering), count and dimensions, condition, and whether it was installed then removed (L191-386).
- **True cost of waste** = quantity x (materials + labour + plant rate) + skip hire + VAT + replacement of items wasted after installation (L396-496, L878-888).
- Demolition waste carries zero purchase cost (L218-237).
- Six condition classes: as good as new; potentially reusable; slight damage or repairable; suitable for recycling; mostly recyclable; disposal (L339-351).

**Informs Tallyard.** A "true cost of waste" or "avoided disposal cost" card for the consultant. Condition class wording.

**Categories.** 7, 9, 17, 18.

## D43 and D45 Zero Waste Scotland case studies (Lovell Homes, Kier)

**Content.**
- Lovell, a Glasgow housing site (£10.5m): true cost about £389 per m3 of skipped waste excluding VAT (£466 including VAT), against about £10 per m3 for skip hire alone. 52% would go to landfill.
- Kier, an Edinburgh refurbishment: about £472 per m3 excluding VAT (£566 including VAT), against about £35 per m3 for skip hire. 57% of the sample by volume was demolition waste. Screws and fixings were skipped because of pack sizes, an opening for take-back.

**Use.** Evidence that skip hire hides most of the cost of waste.

**Categories.** 7, 15.

## D44 Site Waste Reduction Protocol

**Identity.** Zero Waste Scotland with Glasgow Caledonian University, 2022. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- How a trained person samples one skip at a time. Scope is purchased construction materials, not demolition (L18-107).
- Safety rules, estimation of dimensions and volumes (L211-645).
- **Condition definitions** (L673-699):
  - "as good as new";
  - "potentially reusable: undamaged, can be used for its original purpose";
  - "slight damage or repairable: chips under 10 mm".
- **"If waste material leaves site or there is a change of ownership it cannot be legally classified as 'reusable'"** under SEPA rules (L697-699).
- KPIs: cost per m3 and per skip; cost, volume and tonnes by material; shares by source and condition.

**Informs Tallyard.** Grade descriptors (A as good as new or potentially reusable; B slight damage under 10 mm). A legal flag: transfers between owners can raise a waste-status question. The English position needs checking.

**Categories.** 7, 2, 9, 17.

## D47 Olympic Park learning legacy

**Identity.** Olympic Delivery Authority, 2011. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- The Authority set 26 environmental targets, engaged suppliers early, used output-based specifications and a balanced scorecard in procurement, and audited delivery (L1-136).
- **At least 20% by value of secondary materials** (L822), which is where London's 20% by value aim comes from.
- More than 90% of construction waste was diverted (L235).
- A supplier's new product was not specified without official literature and warranties: "too risky for the project" (L936-943). The same barrier faces reused products.

**Use.** Background to the 20% aim. Procurement lessons for specifications.

**Categories.** 10, 15.

## Batch synthesis

### Top findings

1. **Realistic reuse shares at building scale are small.** About 1 to 10% by mass for conventional buildings (BedZED 1.3%), against an average in North-West Europe of 1%. Layer bands: structure 1 to 5%, skin 5 to 15%, space plan 10 to 25%.
2. **Reclaimed prices are not far below new.** They are "comparable to, and even competitive with" new, with the aim of cost neutrality. Low fixed ratios conflict with this.
3. **Preparation is the biggest cost.** Brick removal, cleaning and packaging cost about €287 per m3. Tallyard has no line for it.
4. **Who pays for testing depends on the route:** a client provisional sum, the contractor in design and build, or client feasibility tests.
5. **Reuse is written into tenders with a known toolkit:** objective, award criterion (about 10% of score), variants, a unit-price schedule, a review clause, insurance, execution and origin clauses.
6. **Timing follows a set sequence:** ambitions at stage 0 to 1, audit before demolition, consultation before tender, options fixed by end of design, monitoring at each stage.
7. **Storage is the normal bridge** between availability and need.
8. **Report by mass per layer, plus value where policy needs it, and show both.**
9. **Sourcing in the 32 cases:** dealers in about 14, same site in about 12, site to site in 6 to 8. Demand stated in advance creates supply.
10. **Facade evidence is thin:** SEGRO is the only curtain wall example. The Scottish waste regulator says items changing ownership cannot be called reusable.

### Reuse-in-procurement feature set

The pack would be generated from shortlists, listings and project data, adapting the FCRBE clauses to UK law. Legal review is needed.

**Client:**
- objective builder with ambition levels and unit choice;
- route selector and responsibility matrix (who buys, stores, prepares, tests, insures, is liable);
- clause pack;
- award and selection templates;
- budget mechanics (split supply and installation; provisional sum; reclaimed and new unit prices);
- market consultation pack from public listings.

**Architect:** specification clauses per listing in the factsheet 12 structure:
- origin;
- ranges and tolerances;
- patina;
- operations;
- regulatory vs contractual requirements;
- acceptance;
- presumed quantity;
- surplus;
- storage;
- new fallback;
- CE reserved for new;
- the existing caveat.

**Consultant:**
- monitoring table by RIBA stage;
- factsheet 18 report with mass rates per layer next to value;
- carbon boundary note;
- outflow rate separate from diversion.

**Donor owner or contractor:**
- dismantling clauses;
- dismantling trial;
- storage and packaging;
- batch identity sheet with quantity after dismantling;
- outgoing reuse target.

**Main contractor:** quality plan template; provenance evidence; log of market searches to support a substitution request.

**Testing companies:** test scope templates and their place in the provisional sum.
