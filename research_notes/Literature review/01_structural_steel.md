## D02 DISRUPT: Business considerations for steel reuse

**Identity.** ASBP for the DISRUPT project (Delivering Innovative Steel ReUse ProjecT, Innovate UK 10028038), with Cleveland Steel and Tubes, NFDC and ISG as partners and Grosvenor supporting, 2023. An industry toolkit built from interviews and London case studies. Authority medium to high: grounded in named UK projects, though not peer reviewed, and a stockholder partner has a commercial interest.

**Summary.** For each of eight roles (demolition contractor, stockholder, steel contractor or fabricator, erector, main contractor, cost consultant or project manager, designer, client; L15-24), the toolkit sets out technical, supply chain, economic and carbon considerations. Examples come from Holbein Gardens, Meridian Water, Brent Cross Town Primary Substation, Entopia, 55 Great Suffolk Street and Elephant and Castle. It restates the SCI P427 admissibility rules, gives practical yield, timing, cost and warranty guidance, and shows how ownership and liability differ between client-driven and stockholder-driven supply.

**Key content.**
- P427 admissibility (L66-89, L547-567):
  - steel no older than 1970;
  - no built-up or spliced members unless they are disassembled or their welds are tested;
  - corrosion section loss over 5% of element thickness is significant;
  - no fire exposure and no sign of plasticity;
  - members within BS EN 1090-2 geometric tolerances (straightening allowed).
- Data P427 needs per member (L569-574): building age, original drawings, steel grade, mechanical properties, and the properties to declare under BS EN 1090-2.
- The protocol is being extended to steel back to 1932 (L92-95). About 50% of London's steel is encased in concrete (L98-105).
- Pre-demolition audits are required by the GLA for major developments, by an independent third party, with a justification for demolition and a Circular Economy Statement (L62-75). BREEAM also needs a third-party audit (L78-79). NFDC DRG116:2019 is the industry audit protocol (L84-88).
- Yield (L91-97, L145-153):
  - cutting at the ends is the most efficient recovery;
  - about 70% of mass is recovered and about 15% sits in connections;
  - members are cut about 1 m from each connection.
- Meridian Water specification (L117-124): no end plates or connections; lengths over 3 m and no more than 12 m; undamaged, not bent, not dynamically loaded.
- Unbolting at Holbein Gardens needed scaffold support, at significant cost (L156-199).
- Recover, test, sort, inspect, clean and refabricate took about 6 to 8 weeks (L236-246).
- £40/t estimated for a demolition contractor to put steel into a holding area (L273-275). Central London sites have very little set-down space (L264-271).
- Long sections may need extendable trailers and swept-path checks (L286-293).
- Payment and ownership:
  - payment for reusable steel can equal or exceed scrap (L304-308);
  - in the client-driven model the demolition contractor is paid for labour, not material; in the stockholder model the contractor owns the steel and sells it at a rate set by length, condition and information (L323-332);
  - no established payment benchmarks exist, and inexperienced firms add a risk premium (L334-343).
- Carbon: most of the benefit is credited to the receiving building; the donor's module D credit is small (L389-409).
- Stockholders:
  - need product liability insurance, reservation contracts (deposits, storage fees) and NDT kit (L423-452);
  - usually oversee testing, done by accredited labs in about a week (L522-531);
  - EU testing costs about three times UK testing (L677-679).
- An Elliott Wood and Cleveland Steel grading proposal prefers long lengths and penalises irregular sizes (L464-473). Provenance "is crucial" (L540-545). Shot blasting is the norm (L588-593).
- Warranty and liability chain:
  - the fabricator gives the manufacturing warranty and relies on the stockholder's product liability insurance; the engineer carries professional indemnity (L606-611, L908-916);
  - the client-driven model is ambiguous (L613-621, L922-928);
  - steel made before 2014 needs no CE or UKCA mark to be used in a marked structure, provided its properties are verified by testing (L896-902).
- Two kinds of stock: overstock and cancelled orders, and steel from buildings (L631-639). Reclaimed steel may be reserved and stored for years, with storage fees (L662-667, L689-694).
- Fabrication with existing holes and stiffeners is 15 to 20% slower (L800-803). Reclaimed beams can be 10 to 20 mm deeper than nominal (L1752-1759). Avoid stock that would be under-used by 30% or more (L1723-1725).
- Brent Cross:
  - steel reserved early locked a price while new prices rose (L1324-1329);
  - later value engineering made some pre-sourced steel irrelevant (L1554-1557);
  - a domestic job got stock in 4 days (L1343-1345).
- Entopia assumed a 25% premium and came out cost neutral (L1499-1503).
- Matching tools already exist (HTS, WSP; L1676-1710). Tall-building main frames need extra testing (L1704-1706).
- 55 Great Suffolk Street: 386 kgCO2e/m2 A1-A5, 36% below the LETI 600 target (L2316-2319).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Capture gains the P427 admissibility fields and data list.
- The matcher gains default length limits of 3 to 12 m, a depth tolerance and an under-utilisation warning.
- £40/t is one component of the recovery premium.
- The timeline rule should count from the deconstruction date.
- The donor/recipient carbon rule is confirmed.
- Title to arisings depends on the supply model (founder's question 12).
- Warranty and insurance roles can be shown on each deal.

**Contradictions.** Tallyard ties recoverability to connection type (bolted A, welded B); the toolkit says cutting near connections is often more efficient than unbolting (L142-147). Tallyard's matcher never cuts stock; practice cuts routinely and values long pieces because they can be cut further (L1860-1862).

**Gaps.** No testing, storage, handling or haulage rates. No numeric acceptance tolerances. Who carries indemnity in the client-driven model is left open.

**Feature ideas.**
- Deconstruction specification generator.
- Recoverable length and yield calculator.
- Exposed-steel flag.
- Hole-marking note in the specification export.
- Reservation contract with deposit, storage fee and price lock.
- Warning that value engineering can strand a forward purchase.

**Categories.** 4, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 18.

## D03 DISRUPT guidance note 1: why source reclaimed structural steel?

**Identity.** ASBP, 2023, two-page note. Authority medium.

**Summary and content.**
- Carbon saving "can be up 96%" and cost saving "20% or more", both after rework and testing (L6-9).
- Referable London schemes need a whole life-cycle carbon assessment and a Circular Economy Statement (L31-35). 82% of councils have declared a climate emergency (L28-29).
- The Construction Playbook asks for whole-life carbon in tenders (L57-64). BREEAM and LEED give credits (L66-68).

**Informs Tallyard.** This is the main source for the 20% discount and says it is net of rework and testing. If Tallyard's 0.8 ratio represents it, adding testing on top in buyer comparisons double counts. 96% matches 0.047 against 1.74.

**Gaps.** No breakdown of the 20% and no price year.

**Categories.** 2, 8, 13, 18.

## D04 DISRUPT guidance note 2: technical considerations

**Identity.** ASBP, 2023. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- The usual route is inspect, test, shot or sand blast, refabricate and prime, then CE mark under EN 1090; stockholders offer these services (L14-20). Steel essentially has to be from after the 1970s (L20-23).
- A visual grade from Prime (new) and then A to E, based on holes, fittings and appearance, was proposed by an engineer and a stockholder (L25-29). The BCSA model specification and the FCRBE steel sheet are cited (L31-34); IStructE guidance for designers (L45-46).

**Informs Tallyard.** The A to C condition scale should map onto, or be replaced by, this industry scale. "Certified" should mean CE or UKCA marked by a fabricator under EN 1090.

**Gap.** "There is not as such a visual grading system as yet" (L25): no thresholds.

**Categories.** 3, 4, 16.

## D05 DISRUPT guidance note 3: how and when to source

**Identity.** ASBP, 2023. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- Post-1970s steel; new surplus also qualifies (L6-9).
- Stockholders buy, process, test and sell (L13-18). A donor building rarely supplies enough, so client-driven and stockholder supply are combined (L25-27).
- Securing stock early brings storage cost and design-change risk (L29-34). Pricing is uncertain because yield varies with connections (L36-38).
- Audit quantities are uncertain when steel is hidden (L43-45). Procurement must not penalise the demolition contractor (L51-53).

**Informs Tallyard.** Validates hybrid supply. Capture should flag quantities as seen or inferred (concealed). Forward sale needs storage and design-change terms.

**Categories.** 6, 10, 11, 13.

## D06 DISRUPT literature review

**Identity.** ASBP, April 2022 to March 2023, a referenced grey-literature review. Authority medium to high.

**Summary.** The review covers:
- carbon;
- UK reuse rates (13% in 2000, about 7% of structural steel in 2012);
- new and scrap price trends;
- supply forecasts;
- economics, where Dunant finds reclaimed steel 3 to 10% dearer yet 75% of cases profitable;
- barriers and enablers;
- the full P427 process from audit to CE marking and data recording.

**Key content.**
- Carbon:
  - reuse saves 96 to 97% against virgin steel, recycling about 80% (L114-124);
  - the EMR reusable steel EPD gives 46.6 kgCO2e/t, made up of A1 25.5, A2 13.4 and A3 7.7 (L548-550);
  - the new-steel figure of 1,740 kgCO2e/t is described as "considering A1 module only" (L552-553);
  - a second EMR figure of 15 kg/t has unclear scope (L540).
- Prices: average new beams £428/t in 2016 to £1,307/t in 2022; heavy scrap (grade OA) £65/t to £350/t (L460-463).
- Composition: structural sections are about 25% of a building's steel (L175-177). Steel frames are about 70% of UK multi-storey frames (L193).
- P427 excludes (L196-208):
  - pre-1970 steel (different chemistry, and red lead primer is carcinogenic);
  - steel with a fatigue history;
  - members with plastic hinges, section loss or fire damage;
  - radioactive steel.
- P427 restricts where reclaimed steel can go: not fatigue structures, plastic design or seismic-resisting roles, and extra testing for consequence class CC3 (L210-221).
- About 80% of UK scrap is exported (L263-267). Steel moved within the UK emits under 10 kgCO2/t, against 160 for EU imports and 550 for imports from China (L799-808).
- Dunant 2018: reclaimed steel "can be slightly (3 to 10%) more expensive than new" (L880-882), yet 75% of UK cases were profitable (L892-894). BedZED paid £300/t in 2001 (L917-920).
- Reuse saves money when testing is limited, the donor is near, no refabrication is needed and purchase is cheap (L937-944).
- Barriers: availability, traceability, time, incentives (BREEAM does not reward reuse directly, L986-992), economics.
- Enablers: permanent marking per section (L1041-1043), supplier database (L1046-1049), passports, rapid NDT (L1119-1120).
- Process (L1130-1287):
  - stockholder inspection rejects section loss over 5%, plastic strain and untested welds; dimensions are checked against NSSS tolerances;
  - grading table:

    | Grade | Meaning |
    |---|---|
    | A | Clean, no holes or fittings |
    | B | Holes repaired |
    | C | Some holes to repair |
    | D | Holes remain |
    | E | Not structurally compliant |

  - reconditioning removes coatings containing lead, cadmium or asbestos (L1213-1223);
  - independent accredited labs test yield, ultimate strength, elongation, impact if needed and carbon equivalent (L1225-1244);
  - mandatory CE or UKCA declarations are strength, elongation, tolerances, delivery condition and carbon equivalent if welded (L1247-1266);
  - records cover the building, each member with its group ID, coating and test results, and the stockholder (L1271-1287).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Validates both carbon factors (subject to the module check).
- Replaces the £1,000/t and £220/t placeholders with dated, indexed ranges.
- Adds hazardous-coating fields (feeding WELL X01).
- Supplies a ready-made data schema.

**Contradictions.**
- The 0.8 ratio sits against evidence from 1.10 times new (Dunant) to 0.80 times new.
- The basis of 1,740 (A1 only, or A1-A3) needs checking against the source.
- BREEAM does not reward reuse directly, so Tallyard should map reuse through Mat 01 life-cycle impact rather than a reuse credit.

**Gaps.** Costs of deconstruction, reconditioning, storage and testing are "highly variable" and not given (L999-1001). No P427 sampling frequencies.

**Feature ideas.** Price index panel; P427 data schema with group IDs; testing-plan generator; QR or ID marking; destination restrictions on demand projects.

**Categories.** 2, 3, 4, 8, 11, 17, 18.

## D10 DISRUPT supply chain models

**Identity.** ASBP, 2023. Authority medium.

**Content.** Four models:
- business-as-usual recycling;
- stockholder-driven: stockholders buy from demolition contractors, blast, test and certify;
- client-driven: the client keeps ownership from donor to recipient, information is usually available, design is limited to donor sizes;
- hybrid (L4-43).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Tallyard's principal model (platform buys at 0.55 x new) is the stockholder model; agency is closest to brokering client-driven or hybrid supply.
- Each deal should carry a model field that sets title, testing responsibility, warranty chain and storage.
- Stockholders buy at rates anchored to scrap, so a 0.55 purchase price is not evidenced.

**Categories.** 11, 13.

## D16 ADVANCE D2.1: circular economy of steel-based building components

**Identity.** ADVANCE project deliverable D2.1, Research Fund for Coal and Steel grant 101112269, May 2024. Authors: Hradil, Fülöp and others (VTT) with ECCS, Coimbra, Timișoara, RWTH Aachen, CTU Prague, CTICM, ArcelorMittal and others (L13-45). Authority high on standards (the authors sit on CEN committees TC 135, TC 250 and TC 350) and medium on economics.

**Summary.** A state-of-the-art review of:
- audits, deconstruction protocols, acceptance and testing protocols;
- EN 1090-2 and the new Construction Products Regulation;
- design for reuse;
- LCA methods and circularity indicators;
- about 40 case studies, business models, policy and digital trends.

Its conclusion: reuse of post-1970 steel is feasible with safety factors around 1.1, and steel will be the first material with a full set of reuse standards.

**Key content.**
- The biggest barriers are material qualification and design uncertainty (L218-221). Audits should be commissioned by the owner from a qualified auditor (L228-247).
- PROGRESS audit report template (L343-358): checklists, then parts A to D. Part C, the inventory of reusable components, records dimensions, quantity, location, year, reuse decision, manufacturer, grade, testing procedure and recommended treatment.
- Over 80% of single-storey steel buildings is reusable (L367-369).
- Testing protocols:
  - Norwegian Steel Association (2024, L549-563):
    - A: NDT only, for uncertified steel in EXC1, or EXC2 below S355;
    - B: NDT plus tensile tests, for uncertified S355 and above in EXC2, or EXC3;
    - C: visual only, with certificates and full traceability;
    - D: spectrometer, with certificates but no traceability.
  - Swedish MVR (L631-655):
    - A: visual only, with known provenance and documents;
    - B: test 1 member per group;
    - C: 3 per group;
    - D: test all members.
  - PROGRESS: reclaim in groups of the same form, size, function and source (L603-610).
  - SCI P440 (2023) covers UK steel from 1932 to 1970 (L661-667).
- EN 1090-2 clause 5.1 properties to specify for reused products (L755-769). The new EU CPR (April 2024) defines used and remanufactured products, adds the year of de-installation to the CE mark and requires passports (L778-815).
- LCA:
  - EN 15804+A2 Annex D (L1003-1009);
  - Finland reports reuse benefit as a "handprint" (L1021-1029);
  - France's RE2020 counts reused components as zero (L1039-1041).
- Level(s) indicator 2.4 coefficients (L1148-1159):

  | Route | Coefficient |
  |---|---|
  | Direct reuse | 1.00 |
  | Preparing for reuse | 0.90 |
  | Pure-stream recycling | 0.75 |
  | Mixed-stream recycling | 0.50 |
  | Material recovery | 0.25 |
  | Energy recovery | 0.15 |
  | Landfill | 0.01 |
  | Hazardous disposal | 0.00 |

- BRE design for deconstruction scores (0, 0.5, 1, weighted by embodied carbon) and the PROGRESS reusability index (L1170-1211).
- UK cases (L1662-1756):
  - 55 Great Suffolk Street: 97% reused;
  - Elephant and Castle: 96 of 372 t;
  - Holbein: 24 t;
  - Sloane Square House: 100%;
  - Brent Cross: 45% reuse, about 40% carbon saving;
  - Olympic stadium: 2,500 t of pipeline tube.
  - Failures: Meridian Water drumsheds (time, cost, pre-1970 steel), and a 1950s frame encased in concrete (L1887-1904).
- The EU Taxonomy draft proposed at least 50% reused, recycled or renewable content (15% reused, 15% recycled), but the final version dropped building-level targets (L2349-2367).
- CEN/TS WI 135033 (L2424-2513):
  - Data to collect:
    - date and place of construction;
    - fabricator, erector, designer;
    - drawings and EN 10204 inspection documents;
    - calculation notes and loading history;
    - owner's and O&M manuals;
    - inspection records, interventions and incidents.
  - Minimum record: donor use and **geographical location**, age, component list and condition evaluation.
  - Visual condition evaluation: plastification, fire signs, local damage, holes, cracks, corrosion.
  - Protocols:
    - A: post-1970 steel with certificates; yield estimated.
    - B: post-1970 without certificates; the member with the lowest hardness is destructively tested.
    - C: pre-1970 with certificates; lowest and highest hardness plus at least one random member.
    - D: pre-1970 without certificates; all members.
  - All destructive tests by an independent accredited party with an EN 10204 3.1 equivalent certificate.
- CEN TC 250 SC3 eight-step process chain; reliability index 3.8 for CC2; "safety factors in the 1.1 range" for post-1970 steel (L2519-2767).
- Glossary (L3675-4149):
  - minor alteration is no more than 10% change of major dimensions;
  - **downcycling and upcycling are both forms of recycling**;
  - reuse in situ and relocated reuse;
  - used, remanufactured and reused product.
- Cases:
  - Petite Maison: QR codes and standard bolt grids (L4243-4397);
  - Gennevilliers: test groups formed from hardness readings; some bolts would not loosen (L4675-4729);
  - Romania, 2019 prices (L4885-4972):
    - reused structure bought at €570/t;
    - derived all-in cost about €1,160/t;
    - steel package 17% cheaper than new;
    - project 9% cheaper.
  - Helsinki hall: sold at auction for €440,000; 9-week deconstruction (L4998-5151).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- The strongest evidence for redesigning test status (see the synthesis at the end of this file).
- A claim of S355 needs tensile tests; S275 can rest on NDT.
- Pre-1970 steel routes to P440 rather than being rejected.
- The Romania case supports 0.8 as an all-in ratio, not as an as-found purchase price.
- Level(s) coefficients can weight decision-tree routes.
- Carbon regime notes (RE2020, handprint).

**Contradictions.**
- Tallyard's tree places upcycle and downcycle between reuse and recycle; ADVANCE defines both as recycling.
- Minor alteration allows cutting.
- DIN 1050 (1947) used a capacity factor of 0.8 for rusted sections, which suggests condition should derate capacity rather than price.

**Gaps.** CEN safety-factor tables were lost in extraction. Whether the EU CPR used-product rules apply in Great Britain needs checking. No UK cost data.

**Feature ideas.**
- Test-protocol wizard.
- Group manager with hardness map.
- Certificate record.
- Level(s) route coefficient.
- QR or ID labels.
- Auction mode for whole structures.
- Buy-back clause.

**Categories.** 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 15, 16, 17.

## D11 Circular Buildings Coalition: Building confidence (insurance deep-dive)

**Identity.** Circular Buildings Coalition (WorldGBC, Metabolic, Circle Economy, WBCSD, Ellen MacArthur Foundation, Arup), written by Structural Collective and Metabolic with input from an insurer and reuse platforms, June 2025 (L5-122). Authority medium.

**Summary.** The "insurance issue" covers four risks:
- underperformance;
- delay and cost overrun;
- damage and liability in removal and transit;
- supply reliability.

Existing covers (property, all risks, professional liability, delay in start-up) fit poorly. Current workarounds:
- conservative design;
- cascading use;
- data demands;
- vertical integration;
- sourcing only against known demand;
- futures contracts.

It proposes risk tiers by building layer and process attestation.

**Key content.**
- EU reuse could cut 4.4 to 12.6 MtCO2e from 2025 to 2050 (L141-142).
- Market model: bricks are 96% of mass; about 25% of bricks are recoverable because only the outer leaf qualifies (L418-431, L511-515).
- Engineers bear more hours and professional indemnity exposure, making engineering a bottleneck (L531-548).
- A French reuse platform runs (L580-611):
  - a basic guarantee on every transaction;
  - an optional paid cover up to €500,000 per year;
  - a cover for its own reconditioned products up to €300,000 per year.
  - Structural components and items critical to facade watertightness are excluded.
- Contractors hesitate to disclose secondary materials for fear of losing cover (L629-695).
- Futures contracts lock quantities and prices and inventory buildings well before deconstruction (L863-920).
- Risk by layer (L929-976):
  - Structure: proof by certification and testing.
  - Skin: product liability plus quality assurance.
  - Space plan: supplier warranty or product as a service.
- Sampling works for bulk material only with chain-of-custody transparency (L987-1064).
- A French insurer covers reuse when structural elements are validated by the manufacturer or a design office (L1212-1217). The CTICM steel reuse guide is accepted by insurers (L1236-1247). A Belgian "Safety in Circularity" attestation certifies processes, initially for raised floors and sanitary items (L1253-1281). A German tiered insurance add-on (L1307-1330).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Structure families (steel, timber) need a test or certification status before reservation.
- Skin families (curtain wall, precast, stone, brick) need a QA record.
- Raised floor can rest on a supplier warranty.
- Brick recovery defaults to about 25%.
- Forward sale is validated. The provenance pack can serve as the disclosure file for the client's insurer.

**Contradictions.** EU Taxonomy targets are presented as current (L373-377); ADVANCE says the final Taxonomy dropped them. "Downcycling" here means reuse in a lower-grade application. The 25% brick figure contrasts with the 97% yield of a specific technique in D01.

**Gaps.** No UK insurer, premiums or structural-steel product. Who pays for extra assessment is left open (L273-276).

**Feature ideas.** Assurance tier by family; insurer disclosure pack; process attestation badges; platform guarantee with optional insured extension; harvesting map; capture of claims data.

**Categories.** 2, 11, 12, 13, 18.

## D29 Lignum: warranties, guarantees and insurance

**Identity.** Lignum Risk Partners (London), by an insurance practitioner, June 2026 (L1-31, L404-409). Authority low to medium: experienced, but without data or cases.

**Content.**
- A warranty (designer or contractor to owner, on quality) differs from a guarantee (manufacturer or supplier, on repair or replacement) (L70-75). Either is only as good as the provider's solvency; claims take months and the customer funds the loss meanwhile (L77-91, L217-267). Insurance helps only if the policy mirrors the terms (L109-127).
- Steel reuse "is on the way to becoming a relatively well-established practice": provenance can usually be verified and protocols exist (L178-182). Other materials are seen as high risk (L185-191).
- Needs: provenance; standards for recovery, testing and recertification; a recoverable chain of liability, including testing and certification bodies (L195-306).
- Proposes a community of interest to set shared standards, and alternative risk transfer (L314-352).

**Informs Tallyard.** A chain-of-custody log per item, and a statement on each deal of which instrument applies and who gives it. Steel is the most insurable family.

**Categories.** 12, 10.

## D01 Benachio, Freitas and Tavares (2020), systematic review

**Identity.** Journal of Cleaner Production 260 (2020) 121046 (L1-34). 45 papers to May 2019. Authority medium: peer reviewed but descriptive.

**Content.**
- Construction is over 30% of resource extraction and 25% of solid waste (L38-39).
- Module D contributes only about 3 to 4% of LCA results (L1051-1056). EN 15804 and 15978 do not credit design for disassembly (L1034-1040).
- Demountable steel produces the most reusable material (L1534-1537). A BIM-linked repository brings reuse into design (L1734-1741).
- A UK technique recovered about 97% of bricks (L1746-1747).
- Seven data categories for circularity potential (L1758-1763): composition, quality, path of use, performance time, connections, dimensioning, quality of registration.
- Research gaps: standard reuse practices, passports, business models (L1110-1152).

**Informs Tallyard.** Supports never netting module D. Gives an item data-completeness score. Supports IFC export.

**Categories.** 1, 8, 9, 17.

## Batch synthesis

### Top findings

1. **Test status should depend on provenance, age and execution class.** The four statuses follow the P427 sequence (inspect, test, mark) but ignore these three factors, which decide how much testing is needed. Every protocol branches on them (D16 L539-563, L631-655, L2471-2513).
2. **Testing is done by group, not by piece:** 0, 1, 3 or all members per group of the same form, size, function and source. The per-piece fee model should be restructured.
3. **The P427 admissibility screen belongs at capture,** and destination restrictions belong on demand projects.
4. **Reclaimed steel price:** 20% below new is described as net of rework and testing (D03). The wider evidence runs from 3 to 10% dearer (Dunant), through cost neutral, to 17% cheaper (ADVANCE Romania). Present 0.8 as an all-in benchmark and do not add testing on top.
5. **Title depends on the supply model:** client-driven, stockholder-driven or hybrid.
6. **Physical yield is about 70% of mass,** with cuts about 1 m from connections and usual lengths of 3 to 12 m.
7. **Programme:** recover, test and refabricate takes 6 to 8 weeks; a lab about a week; tested stock 4 days.
8. **Liability chain:** fabricator warranty, stockholder product liability, engineer indemnity. Structure needs test proof; skin needs product liability and QA; space plan needs a warranty.
9. **Provenance cannot be anonymous to technical parties:** CEN/TS requires the donor's location in the test record. Public listings can stay anonymised while the engineer, test house, fabricator and insurer see full data under the deal.
10. **Carbon values are validated:** EMR EPD 46.6 kg/t against new 1,740, an avoided 1.693 t/t. The basis of 1,740 (A1 only, or A1-A3) needs checking.

### Recommended test-status scheme

Store two fields and derive a display label.

**Provenance**

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P0 | Unknown |
| P1 | Known donor, post-1970, no certificates |
| P2 | Known donor with EN 10204 documents traceable to members (overstock usually here) |
| P3 | Donor built 1932 to 1970; routes to SCI P440 |

**Assessment**

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| S0 | Captured only |
| S1 | P427 visual and dimensional inspection: section loss, fire, plasticity, tolerances, coatings, group ID |
| S2 | NDT screening (hardness, spark spectrometry) to confirm groups |
| S3 | Destructive tests to the group sampling rule, with a 3.1 certificate |
| S4 | CE or UKCA marked under EN 1090-2 by the fabricator |

**Display labels**

| Label | Rule | Grade usable in the matcher |
|---|---|---|
| Untested | S0 | Unknown; conservative assumption only, with the engineer's agreement |
| Inspected | S1 or S2 | S275 at most where NDT supports it |
| Tested | S3, or P2 with S1 and a hardness check | Grade from test or certificate |
| CE/UKCA marked (EN 1090-2) | S4 | Declared grade |

**Rules this implies**
- S355 for EXC2 or EXC3 members needs S3.
- Pre-1970 steel without certificates means every member is tested.
- CC3 or tall-building frames need extra testing.
- Rather than price multipliers, price an item at its current status and show the buyer the cost and time to reach the status their member needs.
- Still to verify in the P427 text itself: its default conservative properties, sampling frequencies and partial factors.
