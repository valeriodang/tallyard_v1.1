# Literature review 02: FCRBE reuse toolkit, material sheets

Document D27, `FCRBE-all_sheets_merged-EN.txt` (25,647 lines), read in full. Line references (L123) are to that file. Price conversions marked "derived" use EN 10365 masses and about £0.86 to the euro (2021). Categories: 4, 5, 7, 10, 11, 15.

**The toolkit has no sheet for curtain walling, glazing, windows, aluminium frames or precast cladding panels.** Two of Tallyard's seven families therefore have no direct source here. The concrete shear wall sheet is the nearest proxy for precast.

## 3. Sheets for Tallyard's families

### Steel beam (2.30), L6335-7393

- **Scope:** hot-rolled IPN, IPE, HEA, HEB, HEM, UPE and UPN to EN 10365; grades S235, S275 and S355 (L6398-6537). Finishes range from bare to galvanised to fire protection, which may contain asbestos (L6574-6630).
- **Removal:** careful removal of recent standard beams is "usually easy" (L6654-6666).
  - Desk research on date, use, loads, fire, profile, grade and coating (L6693-6769).
  - Bolted joints are dismantled or cut close to the connection. Critical welds need lifting support. Mark each member on the web; sort by grade (L6694-6767).
  - Recoverability mapping: A bolted or riveted; B welded; C built into masonry or with flocked fire protection.
- **Do not reclaim** (L6657-6690):
  - members with extreme loads, impact or fire history;
  - fatigue use (lift shafts, cranes, bridges);
  - corrosion section loss over 5%;
  - visible plastic deformation;
  - pre-1970 steel unless analysed and tested.
- **Condition and design:**
  - Old holes, fittings and surface rust are usually not a problem. New holes need about 100 mm clearance from existing ones (L6812-6814, L7017-7023).
  - Do not rely on the original fire coating (L6793-6828).
- **Tests** (L7044-7232):
  - Documented grade with visual inspection may be enough; otherwise test.
  - Consequence and execution classes (EN 1090-2) drive verification (L7067-7070).
  - Dimensions (EN 10365) and tolerances (EN 10034); Charpy toughness; tensile (EN ISO 6892-1); portable hardness to estimate strength (EN ISO 18265).
  - Chemistry and carbon equivalent by spectrometry; metallography; coating condition.
  - Group identical beams into test batches during removal (L6784-6803).
  - PROGRESS, SCI P427 and other guides are cited (L7237-7271).
- **Storage:** outdoors off the ground, no water traps, in listed batches (L6860-6889). Transport is costly, so dealers often sell from the demolition site.
- **Availability:** HEA, HEB and IPE are common, in small and medium quantities (L7286-7294). Scale for identical 4 to 6 m elements: Frequent 1 to 5, Occasional 5 to 20, Rare 20 to 100 (L7293-7310).
- **Price, 2021:** €10 to €120 per linear metre by profile (L7297-7318). Derived: about €850 to €1,080/t, median about €960/t (about £730 to £930/t). Prices track scrap.
- **Carbon, new (A1-A3):** 1.41 (INIES CTICM), 4.76 (INIES generic), 1.74 (IBU bauforumstahl), 1.55 (ICE) kg/kg (L7333-7360). No reclaimed factor.
- **Regulation:** CE marking of new structural steel has been compulsory since 2014. EN 1090-2 admits constituent products such as reclaimed steel "if their relevant properties are specified" (L6475-6506).
- **Hazards:** red lead primer, banned around 1990, needs chemical stripping (L7322-7327); asbestos flocking (L7329-7331).
- **Specification advice:**
  - Involve the engineer early.
  - Specify a range of profiles and a minimum length; split batches; allow new steel at key points.
  - Check carbon equivalent before welding. Not for fatigue, seismic or high-ductility uses (L6950-7022).
- **Cases:** BedZED 95 t (L6908); a Winterthur building with 70 t, no tests, the lowest grade assumed (L7374-7380).

### Solid structural timber (2.10), L4550-5471

- **Market:** "technical" softwood joists and beams, plus old oak beams sold for appearance (L4666-4702). Glulam, CLT and LVL are excluded.
- **Sizes:** beams 150 x 150 to 450 x 450 mm, commonly up to 8 m; joists 60 x 150 to 80 x 240 mm; dealers prefer 2.4 to 2.8 m (L5283-5325).
- **Surveys** (L4743-4835):
  - Visual: species, creep, knots, splits, fungi and insects, damp, rusted fixings.
  - Simple tests: mallet, screwdriver, resistograph, moisture meter.
  - Further: coring, lab identification, hazardous substances.
- **Process:** accept a loss percentage; group and number similar elements (L4846-4858). Then de-nail, clean, dry, treat, saw, plane and finish (L4862-4998).
- **Specification items** (L5091-5115): no rotten knots, shakes, rot or large splits; a reasonable number of holes; moisture content; strength class identified.
- **Standards:**
  - Grading under EN 14081-1, visual (EN 518) or machine (EN 519), with classes from EN 1912. A mobile grading machine costs €7,000 to €15,000 (L4913-4944).
  - Moisture 15 to 22% for structure, under 18% for roofs (L5172-5176).
  - Fire class D-s2,d0 without testing at 350 kg/m3 or more and 22 mm or more (L5244-5246).
  - Preservative screening for chromium, lead, copper, arsenic and PCP (L5413-5443).
- **Storage:** on blocks, spaced, sheltered (L5003-5010).
- **Price:** pine joists about €3.50 to €8.50 per metre (derived about €365 to €445/m3); old pine about €350/m3; oak €700 to €2,350/m3; brushing about €20 per metre (L5293-5351).
- **Carbon:** biogenic argument only, no figures.
- **Provenance:** Salvo's "Truly Reclaimed" label was in development (L4930-4941). Some resellers guarantee moisture, dimensions and FSC Recycled origin (L5124-5137).

### Glulam (2.11), L5472-6334

- Mostly reclaimed today for non-structural uses (L5537-5584).
- **Recoverability:** removal by grapple deforms members and rules out structural reuse (L5758-5770). Careful crane removal maps to A or B; grapple to C.
- **Checks:**
  - Moisture 15% maximum (L6050-6055).
  - **Bond durability by delamination or shear test**, with a rapid clamp test (L6121-6148).
  - Service class can step down, never up (L6144-6148).
  - Strength classes GL24 to GL32 (L6159-6172). Fire class D-s2,d0 at 380 kg/m3 and 40 mm (L6205-6208). EN 14080.
- **Storage:** on clean spacers under tarpaulin, opened up for long storage (L5781-5803).
- **Price:** €200 to €450/m3 (L6290).
- **Case:** 13 beams of 16.1 m, inspected and graded GL24h (L6266-6277).

### Solid clay brick (2.40), L8221-9311

- **Background:**
  - Formats include Imperial (UK) 225 x 110 x 65 mm (L8362-8376).
  - Most market stock dates from 1800 to 1970 and was laid in lime mortar. Cement mortar, perforated bricks and lower firing after 1973 reduced reclaimability (L8387-8400).
  - Beware new bricks aged to look reclaimed (L8454-8460).
- **Recoverability** (L8476-8497): lime, clay and bastard mortars come off relatively easily; cement and adhesive mortars are "generally too expensive" to remove. This matches Tallyard's A (lime) and C (cement).
  - Exclude by original use: stables, basements, foundations, cesspools, chimneys (L8482-8485).
  - Run a dismantling trial on several dozen bricks to estimate loss (L8496-8541).
- **Sorting and condition** (L8564-8618):
  - At least one face and one end good.
  - Reject burn marks and ammonia or fuel odours; a dull sound means a crack.
  - Recovery yield is usually 50 to 70% (L8581-8593).
- **Tests:**
  - Site: Karsten tube, moisture, rebound hammer, flat jack, salt strips (L8615-8652).
  - Product standard EN 771-1, with properties split by exposed or protected and loadbearing or not (L8972-9176). Bricks from protected masonry are not recommended outside without frost testing.
- **Storage:** offset-stacked pallets of 500 to 1,000 bricks, no more than 1 m high, wrapped, protected from rain and damp (L8664-8680).
- **Price:** €0.30 to €0.60 per brick hand-moulded, €0.25 to €0.35 extruded (L9190-9216).
- **Carbon:** ICE clay brick 0.45 kg/kg and 2.13 kg per brick. Reclaimed (Gamle Mursten) 0.0027 kg/kg (L9234-9256). A Danish firm obtained CE marking and an EPD for reclaimed bricks (L8715-8723).
- **Specification:** one model per batch; tolerances; colour mixing; about 5% surplus; buy the whole quantity at once; lime mortar for reversibility (L8786-8911).

### Natural stone wall covering slab (2.90), L11982-12934

- **Background:**
  - 30 to 150 cm by 30 to 150 cm, 1 to 8 cm thick (L12058-12063). Often large uniform batches from office, bank and station facades (L12100-12110).
  - Fixing: bonded for 1 to 2 cm slabs, mechanical for 2 to 8 cm. A cover meter finds anchors (L12133-12151).
- **Recoverability:** rail-mounted diamond saw for full joints; with fixings set in mortar studs, sacrifice one slab to gain access; top-down; density over 2.5 t/m3 (L12178-12313).
  - Mapping: A mechanical fixings with open or sealant joints; B clips in mortar studs or full mortar joints; C bonded thin slabs or epoxy.
- **Risk:** thin marble can bow, crack at anchors and fall, especially on sunny facades. Exclude bowed slabs (L12355-12369, L12735-12736). No cracks at fixing points (L12541-12583).
- **Tests:** EN 1469.
  - Properties by rock: sandstone 2,000 to 2,700 kg/m3; granite 2,500 to 3,000; marble 2,600 to 2,900 (L12665-12677).
  - Flexural strength EN 12372 with a breaking-load formula (L12742-12764).
  - **Anchor pull-out EN 13364: at least 200 N indoors, 300 N outdoors** (L12772-12778).
  - Frost EN 12371; thermal and humidity cycles EN 16306 for marble (L12809-12869).
- **Thickness:** 4 cm for mechanically fixed soft limestone, 3 cm for denser stone (L12418-12512).
- **Storage:** on edge in crates; **untreated, tannin-free spacers, no metal straps** (L12324-12342).
- **Price per m2:** €15 to €25 limestone; about €20 granite; about €50 marble; about €120 Belgian blue stone; about €160 Carrara (L12897-12916).
- **Carbon, new:** granite 31.8 kg/m2 (0.6 kg/kg), marble 16.3 (0.3), limestone 14.9 (0.3), for 2 cm at 52 kg/m2 (L12918-12929).
- **Specification:** one rock type and use per batch; thickness rule; dimension ranges; accepted imperfections; a contractual sample and sampling on receipt (L12391-12610).

### Concrete introduction (0.11) and shear wall (2.91): precast proxy

- "Today, concrete is not a material that can be found on the reclamation market"; reuse is project-led (L23195-23222). Facade panels are non-structural infill (L23322-23379).
- **Core properties:** strength class, exposure class and dimensions; for the steel, grade, bar positions and cover (L23392-23453).
- **Diagnosis** (L24770-24911): thickness, Ferroscan, **carbonation depth against cover**, compressive strength, surface pull-off.
  - Acceptance: thickness over 16 cm and within 1 cm; straightness within 1 cm or 5 mm per metre; no through cracks or cracks over 1 mm; reject spalling from rebar corrosion.
  - Carbonation over half the cover means mortar repair (L25494-25500).
- **Recoverability:** heavy plant, top-down. **Tilting a wall off plasticises the bars and rules out structural reuse** (L24927-24971). Precast panels may need only their joints stripped (L24991-25001).
  - Mapping: A precast with dry or bolted joints; B grouted joints; C cast in situ.
- **Applications:** infill (at most 3 panels in a row), monolithic reinforced wall, or masonry-type wall (L25142-25426). EN 1992, EN 14992, EN 13369. Density 2,400 to 2,500 kg/m3.
- **Cost:** in one study (REPAR#2) the reclaimed solution cost about 7% more than new brick and nearly twice new blocks; optimised, about 30% less (L25567-25616).
- **Carbon, new walls:** 20.3 to 37.7 kg/m2 (0.06 to 0.08 kg/kg) (L25632-25646).
- **Hazards:** ground chemical attack; asbestos coatings (L25599-25629).

### Raised access floor (4.40), L17300-17907

- **Background:**
  - Panels 500 or 600 mm square, 16 to 44 mm thick; cores of high-density chipboard or calcium sulphate (L17391-17427).
  - **Pedestals and stringers are rarely reclaimed** (L17403-17421).
  - A label on the underside gives reference, date, load class and fire class (L17444-17477).
- **Assessment:** finish wear, glued finish, warping, corrosion, residual deflection, documentation (L17473-17514).
- **Removal:** lift with a suction cup, not screwdrivers; discard deformed panels; pallets with spacers, 10 to 15 kg per panel (L17464-17505).
  - Mapping: A loose-laid with loose finish; B glued finish; C glued pedestals or heavy glue residue.
- **Certification:** "one of the rare reclamation products that can be accompanied by a performance certification" (L17560-17570). A 22,000 m2 project created a business offering guarantees and carbon assessments (L17545-17558).
- **Storage:** 15 to 25 °C and relative humidity 40 to 65% (L17573-17579).
- **Standards:** no harmonised EN; in the UK, **PSA MOB PF2 and BS EN 12825** (L17707-17711).
  - Reference life 25 years for fibreboard panels (L17725-17727).
  - Deflection class A: residual deflection no more than 0.5 mm after 30 minutes (L17737-17742).
  - Fire, electrical and acoustic checks; tolerance about 0.5 mm (L17745-17803).
  - Formaldehyde (L17821-17831).
- **Availability:** batches of identical panels up to 1,000 m2 regularly available (L17852-17862).
- **Price:** €10 to €20 per m2 for panels only, **"around 85% of the new price"** (L17861-17866).
- **Carbon:** new 42.7 to 95.5 kg/m2; reclaimed panels 1.05 to 1.67 kg/m2 (L17876-17899).

### Ranked new families for a structure-and-facade-first UK marketplace

1. **Natural slate.** UK market most developed; clear standard.
2. **Glulam,** as a timber subtype.
3. **Stone sills, copings and paving,** as stone subtypes.
4. **Precast concrete walls and floors.** Project-sourced; material value can be zero.
5. **Clay roof tiles.**
6. **Carpet tiles,** which come with raised floors from office strip-outs.
7. **Solid timber flooring.**
8. **Fire doors.** Ordinary-door use by default unless recertified.

Radiators, sanitaryware and hardscape fall outside the core focus.

### Knowledge gaps

- **Families with no sheet:** curtain walling, glazing, windows, aluminium, precast cladding, metal cladding, hollowcore, insulation, ceiling tiles, partitions and luminaires.
- **No UK regulation.** UKCA, the Approved Documents, Regulation 7, the Building Safety Act, BS 5534, BS 8298, FD classes, CDM 2015 and the asbestos regulations all need sources from outside the toolkit.
- **Prices:** EUR, 2019 to 2021, few new-price baselines, nothing on London trade prices.
- **Carbon:** reclaimed-side factors only for brick and raised floor.
- **Testing:** no costs and no sampling rates.

### Feature ideas grounded in the sheets

1. **Capture checklist per family** from each sheet's focal points:
   - Steel: profile, markings, date, coating, connections.
   - Brick: model, mortar colour, exposure, original use.
   - Stone: rock, thickness, fixing (cover meter), joints, bowing.
   - Raised floor: label, finish, deflection.
2. **Hazard flags by date:**
   - red lead before about 1990;
   - asbestos flocking;
   - fire doors before 1997;
   - tile adhesives before 1990;
   - tar parquet glue;
   - chipboard formaldehyde before 1990;
   - PCP or CCA timber;
   - lead paint;
   - asbestos-cement slates.
3. **Rarity indicator by batch size,** warning architects when a shortlist needs a rare batch.
4. **Yield and surplus calculator per family.**
5. **Processing state** (as found, cleaned, ready to install) with cost adders. Cleaning roughly doubles tile prices.
6. **Specification clause export** from the introduction templates:
   - removal with storage and packaging conditions;
   - on-site reuse;
   - external supply with origin declaration;
   - presumed quantity;
   - pay only for salvaged area;
   - surplus;
   - contractual sample;
   - tolerated defects.
7. **Storage rules per family:**
   - steel outdoors off the ground;
   - timber covered and spaced;
   - brick pallets no more than 1 m high;
   - stone on edge without metal straps;
   - raised floor and doors at 15 to 25 °C.
8. **Batch and traceability model:** marked elements, test batches, witnessed sampling.
9. **Flexible design fields for architects:**
   - profile ranges and minimum lengths for steel;
   - free-length coursing for stone;
   - wild bond for brick;
   - one batch per floor for raised floors.
10. **Consequence and execution class input** to set steel testing.
11. **Original use and exposure fields** as stand-ins for frost resistance and timber service class.
12. **Provenance and anti-imitation flag.**
13. **Carbon panel** showing the toolkit's range of new-equivalent values with sources.
