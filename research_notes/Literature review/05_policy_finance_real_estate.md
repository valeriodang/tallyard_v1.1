## D41 British Land: Full circle, full potential

**Identity.**
- British Land with GXN, 2024 (L1-39). The project guidance of a major London REIT.
- Authority: high for owner practice; medium for its general statistics.

**Summary.**
- Every project with existing buildings does a pre-redevelopment audit. Every project proposing demolition does a materials mapping audit using a Demolition Material Tracker.
- Every project has a reuse strategy and design-for-circularity principles, and produces passports to a Material Passport Protocol (L560-596).
- Actions and responsibilities are set by RIBA stage and role (L605-805). There are seven tools (L807-830).

**Key content.**
- **Targets:** office embodied carbon down 50% by 2030, to **below 500 kgCO2e/m2 upfront** and **below 275 kgCO2e/m2 in-use embodied** (L184-190).
- **Recovered so far** (L186-218):
  - 2 Finsbury Avenue: about 9,500 m2 of raised floor, 385 tCO2;
  - 1 Triton Square: reused facade panels, 2,400 tCO2;
  - 1 Broadgate: 140 t of steel repurposed, 238 tCO2 on the IStructE factor.
- **Context:**
  - Construction and demolition waste is 62% of UK waste. London buildings hold about 1.5 t of material per m2 (L99-123).
  - Circular Economy Statements have been required since March 2022 and include a **bill of materials** with quantities, expected lifespans and end-of-life scenarios (L144-162).
  - Pre-demolition audits are required by BREEAM and for GLA-referable projects. Content: components and materials, estimated quantities, embodied carbon and end-of-life route on the waste hierarchy. Definitions follow ISO 20887 (L355-392).
- **Recommended end-of-life route by material** (L430-438):
  - direct reuse: steel, raised floor, furniture, carpet tiles;
  - upcycling: contaminated cullet, timber studwork, ductwork, broken brick or stone;
  - recycling: steel, aluminium, glass, bricks;
  - downcycling: concrete, coloured glass, timber;
  - disposal: contaminated materials, chipboard, bonded insulation.
- **Stage actions** (L605-700):
  - The sustainability consultant defines a future circularity metric and target at stages 0 to 1.
  - Circular targets go into tenders and the owner's tracker.
  - Tenants are invited to opt in to circular fit-out.
- **Roles matrix:** owner, project manager, architect, sustainability consultant, cost consultant, design team, contractor, fit-out consultant, fit-out contractor (L725-805).
- **Tools** (L807-985):
  - Circular Economy Statement template, used at stage 1, planning, stage 4 and within 2 months of completion.
  - **Demolition Material Tracker:** started by the consultant at stage 1, updated by the demolition contractor, finalised before demolition, then updated "as demolished" with each stream's final destination.
  - **Deconstruction Tender Template:** contractors' feedback on feasibility, cost, programme and risk for each route.
  - Employer's Requirements for deconstruction and for passports.
  - **Material Passport Protocol:** an Excel file completed by the main contractor at stages 5 to 6, platform-agnostic, due within 2 months of practical completion (L966-985, L1420-1436).
- **Layer lifespans** (L1118-1125):

  | Layer | Lifespan (years) |
  |---|---|
  | Structure | 50 to 150 or more |
  | Skin | 35 to 50 |
  | Services | 15 to 25 |
  | Space plan | 5 to 15 |
  | Stuff | 1 to 8 |

- **Passport fields:**
  - Base data (L1625-1660): GUID, Uniclass code, RICS category, level, material, year, dimensions, count, area, mass, volume, A1-A3 factor, design life, and **reused, recycled, reusable and recyclable content, all by mass**.
  - Steel (L1670-1695): supplier, profile, **grade** ("the most important"), finish ("galvanised cannot be reused or recycled"), fire coating.
  - Glass (L1700-1730): type, treatment (frit hinders recycling), U-value. **"Facade glazing is currently not commonly reused or recycled in the UK market."**
  - Aluminium: alloy and finish.
  - Raised floor (L1765-1800): manufacturer, model, grade, cover type. **"Raised access floor has become a commonly reused product, sent to specialist resellers to get recertified with a new warranty."**
- **Portfolio-first reuse:**
  - Teams review the owner's existing passports before design. Contractors collect from identified projects or the owner's storage and lead recertification (L1505-1560).
  - Tenant fit-out passports are carried through lease agreements (L1446-1475).
  - 2 Finsbury Avenue steps: search, harvest, transport, storage at the manufacturer's centre, design and manufacture, installation (L1565-1600).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- An off-the-shelf passport and specification schema for steel, curtain wall and raised floor.
- A portfolio-first visibility tier.
- Consultant tasks tied to RIBA stages.
- Statement milestones.
- The demolition contractor and tenant as roles.
- Recertification and warranty as a listing attribute.

**Contradictions.**
- The upfront target (under 500) is about half of Tallyard's 950 default.
- Content is measured by mass, not value.
- Bricks are routed to recycling, not reuse.
- Facade glazing is rarely reused, which weakens demand for curtain wall except as whole units (1 Triton Square).
- **Possible double counting:** the same Broadgate steel seems to be claimed here and by a developer in D21 (about 1.7 tCO2/t here against about 2.5 there).

**Gaps.** No costs, storage economics, protocols for timber, brick, stone or precast, or post-reuse warranty terms.

**Feature ideas.**
- Passport protocol export.
- Demolition Material Tracker view with as-demolished destinations.
- Deconstruction tender comparison.
- Employer's Requirements clause library.
- Portfolio stock search.
- Tenant fit-out passport.

**Categories.** 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17.

**Gaps.** No costs or reuse quantities; it predates the final 2022 guidance.

## D21 NLA: Circular London, building a renewable city

**Identity.**
- New London Architecture, July 2023, Net Zero programme, guided by an expert panel (L1-12, L3599-3603).
- Authority: medium. Showcase figures are self-reported answers to an open call (L1437-1440).

**Summary.**
- "London has no functioning second-hand materials market that is easy to procure from" (L88-89).
- Hierarchy: keep the building, refurbish, reuse, and recycle last.
- Statements have been required for referable schemes since March 2022 (L366-383).
- Material chapters cover steel, concrete, timber, masonry, facades, fit-out, glass and MEP.
- Call to action (L214-236, L1006-1150):
  - VAT parity;
  - a mandatory pre-demolition audit in planning;
  - a carbon review panel and demolition impact assessment;
  - Part Z;
  - consistent recertification;
  - a London storage and logistics strategy;
  - local reuse hubs;
  - material leasing.

**Key content.**
- **What a statement contains:** "a table and a written statement". SI 7(A) targets are the minimum; applicants explain each target, how it is met and monitored, and which metrics are used (L366-383).
- **Policy proposals and status:**
  - VAT is 20% on refurbishment against 0% on new build; 5% or less is proposed (L1008-1027).
  - A pre-demolition audit amendment was tabled to the Levelling Up and Regeneration Bill in April 2023 (L1010-1022).
  - Part Z and Approved Document Z are an industry proposal (L1037-1072). LETI proposes a "% of materials reused" indicator (L1053-1066).
  - City of London whole life-cycle carbon optioneering is adopted (L416-419).
  - Brussels makes renovation the default (L444-455).
  - The RIBA stages have no end-of-life stage, and there is "no centralised methodology to measure circularity" (L1157-1171).
- **Industry change:**
  - Demolition contractors might "take the materials for nothing or even buy them" (L1100-1113).
  - Leasing (L1118-1125).
  - A city-scale storage and logistics strategy (L1085-1094).
- **Barriers:**
  - Finding materials in quantity, priced competitively and "certified for safe use against regulations or warranties" (L555-558).
  - Lump-sum procurement (L585-590).
  - A developer cites risk appetite, "the lack of a tech-enabled open marketplace" and a lack of regulation (L1318-1327).
  - CE marking was "a previously immovable barrier" on one project (L2207-2210).
  - Pre-1970 steel is not covered (L1785-1790).
  - A warning against stripping buildings in low-value regions and transporting materials long distances (L593-597).
- **Steel:**
  - The frame is 50 to 60% of a building's embodied carbon (L537-539).
  - New 1,740 against under 50 kgCO2/t reused (L587-590).
  - A rule of thumb: if more than 30% of a member would be excess, use it elsewhere (L580-582).
  - A stock matcher matched 82% of new steelwork on one scheme and returns offcuts to stock (L1863-1868, L3198-3235).
  - Encased members, intumescent coatings and shear studs need more work (L1766-1772).
  - Main stockholders are in northern England (L577-579).
- **Cases:**

  | Project | Result | Lines |
  |---|---|---|
  | 55 Great Suffolk Street | 97% of structural steel reused; 386 kgCO2e/m2 | L1668-1712 |
  | Holbein Gardens | 93% of structure retained | L1720-1772 |
  | TBC.London | 265 kgCO2e/m2 | L2880-2893 |
  | Roots in the Sky | A developer bought 139 t for several projects; donor chosen for a similar grid | L2177-2225 |
  | Meridian Water | 18,000 cement-mortar bricks cleaned; a new **material value retention** metric (CE-MVR) at 50% | L2675-2690 |
  | SEGRO Courier Road | Reclaimed materials with take-back; 3.2 t waste per 100 m2; third-party carbon check | L2229-2256 |
  | Panorama St Paul's | Portland stone and granite in demountable panels | L2125-2160 |
  | Timber Square | Remanufactured raised floor | L3008-3019 |
  | Air Street | Partition glass offered for reuse | L722-731 |

- **Glass:** toughened glass is almost impossible to cut, so melting is the practical route (L782-793).
- **Passports and exchanges:**
  - An open passport with headings: identity, dimensions and location, quantity, properties, circularity (L808-811).
  - A "first material bank" in the City (L2380-2393).
  - Named exchanges and ReLondon's beta portal (L940-967).
  - Enfield lists materials in standing buildings from audits and statements (L966-967).
  - Paris runs a demolition database (L962-968).
- **Futures:** a London firm offers a digital inventory, certification and futures contracts on materials, citing forward planning, visibility of volumes and prices, and liquidity (L1203-1269).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Validates the carbon factors.
- Reclaimed steel should carry a small processing factor.
- Transport legs to stockholders matter.
- Matcher rules: the 30% excess rule and offcuts.
- Forward sale and brokered storage.
- The statement table structure.
- A policy-watch list.
- Passport headings and the CE-MVR metric.

**Contradictions.**
- Exemplar upfront carbon (265 to 386) is far below the 950 default.
- Curtain wall glass is rarely reusable as glass.
- The text says "45% of all structural steel is being re-used" (L623-628), which conflicts with "almost no steel is reused" (L1300). It is probably the DISRUPT 2050 scenario misquoted.

**Gaps.** Showcase figures unverified; no pre-1970 steel protocol; no prices.

**Feature ideas.**
- Stock matching with an excess threshold and offcut return.
- Matching on structural grid.
- An "as found" steel condition flag (primer only, encased, intumescent, shear studs).
- Import of audits and statements.
- CE-MVR metric.

**Categories.** 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18.

## D17 Better Buildings Partnership: driving circularity in the built environment

**Identity.** BBP member briefing, statistics as of April 2025. Authority medium.

**Content.**
- GPE has a "Circularity Score" with a minimum of **40%** for projects starting after April 2025, rising to **over 60% by 2040**. It identified over 1,500 t of steel from one building for reuse in another.
- British Land's strategy and passports (D41).
- Derwent reconditions raised floors on one site and sources refurbished ones; it has partnered with Material Index and CollectEco for a portfolio approach.
- Landsec at Timber Square:
  - retained 80% of the structure;
  - used **115 t of reused steel, cutting about 276 tCO2**;
  - salvaged raised floor tiles from two other projects;
  - is on track for upfront carbon about 50% below a typical office.
- Canary Wharf has had zero waste to landfill from managed areas since 2009. The City of London aims to be fully circular by 2040.
- 7% of BBP members have a dedicated circularity strategy; 54% include it in their ESG strategy.
- BBP Green Lease Toolkit: circular clauses for landlord and tenant works, a recycling of waste clause, and **yield-up provisions** promoting reuse when tenants vacate.

**Informs Tallyard.** Owner circularity scores as dashboard metrics. Portfolio-level reuse. Green lease yield-up as a trigger for fit-out supply.

**Categories.** 13, 15.

## D12 ACAN Circular Economy Policy Campaign 2025

**Identity.** Architects Climate Action Network, June 2025, lead authors from two London practices. Authority medium (an advocacy campaign by practitioners).

**Ten demands.**
1. Extended producer responsibility under the Environment Act.
2. Amend Part A for durability and adaptability, Part D, and the WRAP standards; align with BS 8001; require passports.

   - Part Z;
   - mandatory whole-life carbon assessment with a project tracker through all stages;

   - a desk study and design team meeting before the visit;

9. A national network of reuse hubs for storing, testing, reconditioning and upcycling.

## D25 Don't Waste Buildings: The reuse dividend

**Identity.** A UK volunteer campaign group, late 2025 or early 2026 (cites sources to November 2025). Authority medium to low: advocacy, secondary sources, research done with an AI platform (L61-68).

**Summary.**
- UK VAT is 20% on repair and refurbishment while new homes are zero-rated, "a perverse incentive for demolition" (L93-99, L929-937).
- A review of incentives in eight countries (L313-379).
- "None of the UK schemes specifically reward the reuse of existing structures or the use of reclaimed materials" (L997-1005).
- UK Sustainability Reporting Standards make embodied carbon financially material through Scope 3 (L1075-1145).
- Recommends VAT harmonisation, tax credits (including for reclaimed materials), grants and subsidised finance (L83-108).

**Key content.**
- Proposed "tax credits for using reclaimed materials" and "reused materials credits" (L96, L231-233).
- The Dutch MIA/Vamil investment allowance covers retrofit using reused materials (L359-364, L628-662).
- UK capital allowances exclude structure and fabric (L966-972).
- UK SRS S2 requires Scope 3 disclosure; demolition and rebuild emissions fall under categories 1 and 2; first mandatory reports likely from 2027 (L1102-1120).
- Investors should ask for upfront carbon comparisons and circular criteria (L1190-1240).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- There is no UK fiscal incentive for reclaimed materials today. The levers are planning, BREEAM, corporate targets and, from 2027, Scope 3 reporting.
- Avoided carbon can be shown as avoided Scope 3 emissions.
- A VAT and incentive scenario toggle.

**Contradictions.** Retrofit-first framing raises demand for reclaimed products but can shrink donor supply.

**Categories.** 2, 12, 13, 18.

## D40 Arup and the Ellen MacArthur Foundation: From principles to practices

**Identity.** Arup and EMF with GXN, JLL and RICS, February 2020 (L133-134). Business models tested by discounted cash flow on real projects. Authority medium to high (explicit method, aligned with valuation standards), but feasibility-level and 2020.

**Summary.** Five business models:

| Model | Result |
|---|---|
| Flexible spaces | Net present cost 18% lower |
| Adaptable assets | IRR 3% higher |
| Relocatable buildings | IRR 26% |
| **Residual value** | Net present cost 5% lower |
| **Performance procurement** | IRR 10.8% vs 7.5% |

(L3240-3275.)

**Key content.**
- Accounting depreciation writes materials down faster than they lose value; residual value is "normally captured by the demolition sector, long after the investor has moved on" (L885-910).
- **Residual value model** (L2566-2880):
  - A futures contract on a building's recoverable materials is sold by the client at completion and traded on a central exchange; the holder at deconstruction owns the materials.
  - Enablers include passports and "testing and recertification protocols for pre-used materials".
  - Testbed (Berlin retail fit-out):
    - 82% of materials by spend recoverable;
    - futures value assumed at **50% of original price**;
    - design-for-deconstruction premium 5%;
    - net present cost saving 5% (1% at 25% value, 20% at 125%);
    - **break-even at about 33% of materials by spend sold**.
  - Steps include "notify market of deconstruction date" and "inspect and/or test material specification for reuse". Early adopters: retail fit-out and single-storey steel portal frames.
- **Performance procurement contract terms** (L3140-3165):
  - lease and use rights;
  - length;
  - payment;
  - KPIs with verification;
  - interoperability;
  - data ownership;
  - risk sharing;
  - penalties;
  - insurance;
  - maintenance;
  - break clauses;
  - provider insolvency.
  - End of contract: renew, or the operator takes ownership.
- 46% of demolished UK homes were 11 to 32 years old (L1691). IFRS 16 puts leases on balance sheet (L1077-1110). "Whether financial markets and institutions buy into this concept remains to be seen" (L3374).

**Informs Tallyard.** The strongest support for forward sale, with reference values. Testing referral. Valuation through passports. The design-for-deconstruction premium.

**Contradictions.** The model needs **public notice of deconstruction dates**, against Tallyard's private-pipeline default. Recoverability is measured by spend.

**Categories.** 1, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17.

## D37 Griffiths et al. (2025): hurdles to a circular built environment

**Identity.**
- Buildings 15(8) 1332, 17 April 2025, open access, COST Action CA21103.
- A survey of 270 respondents across Europe, a convenience sample with heavy Romanian participation.
- Authority medium. Figures 10 to 13 are garbled in extraction.

**Content.**
- Barriers ranked: economic and market 38%, policy about a third, technical 17%, cultural about 11% (L129-139).
- About **62% rate insurance and professional indemnity** a significant barrier. Firms want reused products "provided there is a warranty". A German platform created insurance with an insurer, and reclaimed materials "remained 20 to 30% cheaper than new ones" (L1293-1318).
- Collaboration 75%; public procurement 67% (L1349-1402).
- Other barriers reported (L2394-2440):
  - extra cost;
  - no passports;
  - no business models;
  - "a lack of relevant contracts that are able to deal specifically with reused materials";
  - no storage;
  - a programme change that killed a reuse plan.
- DIN SPEC 91484 for audits (L2534-2538). Scotland's public procurement reporting note SPPN 3/2022.
- "Not all construction materials suit such banks and are best removed from one site for immediate reuse elsewhere" (L2649-2655).

**Informs Tallyard.**
- Warranty and insurance status as core listing data.
- Contract templates.

## D15 BASE and SET Alliance: Product-as-a-service and circularity

**Identity.** BASE and the SET Alliance for the Circular Buildings Coalition, 15 February 2024. Authority medium (qualitative, services layer only).

**Content.**
- Product as a service fits the services layer best (L180-252). Circularity "is not inherent" (L196-199).
- Contracts usually run 7 to 15 years for systems over €150,000 (L519-626). There are no criteria for how circular an offer is (L546-549).
- **Provider and customer clauses** (L669-905):
  - KPIs;
  - data access;
  - **ownership**, which must stay off balance sheet under IFRS 16;
  - pricing;
  - length;
  - **take-back and end of contract**: return, extension, early termination, avoiding inappropriate disposal.
- Financier clauses reward products reused or recycled (L915-995). Sub-supplier clauses cover modularity and "percentage of components that can be remanufactured".
- Financiers lack clarity on residual value and secondary markets (L1126-1130).

**Informs Tallyard.** Items in donor buildings may be owned by a service provider, lessor or tenant, so listings need a legal-owner field and any take-back obligation. Secondary markets give residual value visibility.

**Contradictions.** Tallyard sells ownership outright. IFRS 16 could reclassify a forward sale or lease-back.

**Categories.** 1, 10, 11, 12, 17.

## Batch synthesis

### Top findings

1. **The market gap is real, but the binding barriers are not technology.** They are certification, warranty and insurance, plus timing and storage. Warranty, recertification and testing should be core listing data.
2. **Owners' carbon targets are much stricter than Tallyard's default:** British Land under 500 upfront, LETI 600, exemplars 265 to 386. Treat 950 as a compliance ceiling, show benchmark bands and lead with the owner's target.
3. **Every metric needs its basis stated.** British Land content is by mass; 7 Millbank diversion by volume; futures by spend; Meridian Water by value retained.
4. **Avoided carbon needs a declared factor, processing and transport, and no double counting** (the Broadgate steel).
5. **Owners reuse within their own portfolios first.** An "own portfolio" visibility tier partly answers the disclosure question.
6. **Planning already discloses much of what Tallyard keeps private.** The bill of materials and audit become public for referable schemes, so privacy matters most before the application.
7. **Forward sale has a published model but no proof yet.** It also requires public notice of deconstruction dates.
8. **No UK fiscal incentive exists for reclaimed materials.** Planning, BREEAM, corporate targets and Scope 3 reporting from about 2027 are the levers.
9. **Green finance frameworks name Tallyard's activity** (IFC 2025), so Tallyard can produce lender-ready KPIs.
10. **Missing roles:** demolition contractor, tenant, service provider, stockholder. Storage evidence favours partnering.

### Financial and business-model evidence

- **Forward sale:** futures at 50% of original value, break-even at 33% (D40). A London futures service and a developer's multi-project purchase (D21).
- **Valuation:** depreciation understates remaining value (D40). Commodity-based valuation of a materials bank. Residual value unclear to financiers (D15). Insured reclaimed materials 20 to 30% cheaper than new (D37).
- **Insurance:** a German insurance product (D37; the white paper is cited but absent). Raised floor recertified with new warranties (D41).
- **Storage:** an owner's own storage; a City "material bank"; Berlin's publicly backed hub; "not all materials suit banks".
- **Incentives:** none in the UK (D25); the Dutch MIA/Vamil precedent.
- **Green finance:** IFC eligibility; sustainability-linked debt; circular credit lines (D33, D40).
- **No document benchmarks commission or subscription levels**, so Tallyard's 8%, 10% and subscription prices remain unvalidated.
