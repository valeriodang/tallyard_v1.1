1. Project Background 
1.1 The Existing Site 
The application site is partly in the conservation area, the Site includes 326-332 High Holborn & 26 Southampton 
Buildings (Holborn Gate), 319-325 High Holborn (Heron House) and 44 Southampton Buildings. Site is located 
within the administrative boundary of the City of London but borders the London Borough of Camden to the north 
of High Holborn and west of Chancery Lane. The Site is located at the junction of High Holborn and Chancery 
Lane, sitting right on top of Chancery Lane Underground Station. It is an island site bounded by High Holborn to 
the north, Staple Inn Buildings to the east, and Southampton Buildings to the west and south, with 25 
Southampton Buildings fronting the southern facade and Chancery House fronting the western façade. 
The development will replace outdated buildings that no longer meet modern energy efficiency and workspace 
standards. The project aims to achieve BREEAM 'Excellent' certification, targeting an EPC 'A' rating and meeting 
London Plan sustainability requirements. The scheme is designed with future flexibility in mind, ensuring long-
term value and adaptability to future needs. 

1.1.1 History of the Existing Site 
The site of the High Holborn Project has a rich and varied history that dates back several centuries. The buildings 
currently occupying the site were constructed during the 20th century, with Heron House and Holborn Gate being 
built in the 1960s, serving primarily commercial functions and 44 Southampton Buildings being constructed in 
1913-1914, originally built as office space. Southampton Buildings has undergone several renovations over the   
decades. In the 1960s, further developments saw the construction of Holborn Gate and Heron House, both office 
buildings typical of post-war London redevelopment. 

Most of the site including the buildings to the north and northeast are not within a Conservation Area. However, 
the site is surrounded to the south, east and west by the Chancery Lane Conservation Area which includes some 
public realm. 44 Southampton Buildings is the exception on the site and is an unlisted building located within the 
Chancery Lane Conservation Area. The site also holds archaeological significance, as it is situated near the 
historic precincts of the medieval Knights Templar, who established their first temple in this area during the 12th 
century. Excavations conducted in the early 2000s uncovered medieval foundations and remnants of Roman 
inhumations, confirming the site's deep historical roots. The surrounding area is dotted with listed buildings and 
sits adjacent to and partially includes the Chancery Lane Conservation Area, known for its legal, academic, and 
historical importance. 

Although the site has seen multiple renovations over the recent years, the site currently does not meet the modern 
office standards, and the buildings are insufficient for modern use, with the various refurbishments and extensions 
also reducing the original architectural integrity.  

The buildings inefficient use of space and outdated design make them unsuitable for modern commercial needs. 
This development provides a key opportunity to integrate sustainability and historical preservation within the 
framework of modern construction and redevelopment.

In line with the PAN guidance, both qualitative and quantitative assessments of the four options were undertaken. 
This analysis highlighted the following significant constraints to the three retention options: 
• Fire issues: escape routes, fire ratings, cores do not currently meet requirements for fire or evacuation, and 

significant structural interventions to the cores would be required to achieve compliance. 
• Poor thermal performance of the existing façade 
• Limited floor to floor heights, which do not satisfy BCO requirements 
• Non-unform geometry and grids of the existing buildings make the building unsuited for adaptation, limiting the 

scope for vertical/lateral extension. 
• Interfacing of floor slab levels, stair & core access between the retained and the new structure. 
As a result of these investigations alongside the pre-redevelopment audit study, it was determined that whilst it 
may be technically feasible to retain some assets (e.g. in terms of capacity of the structure), there would be 
challenges relating to commercial viability, and the social value of retention scenarios may be limited. Therefore, 
the new build design option was chosen for the Proposed Development which includes the demolition and 
reconstruction of the entire building, utilising the full site area and increasing lettable area. 
The benefits of the new build option include providing a more efficient, future-proof design with optimised 
structural performance, greater flexibility, and sustainability, while overcoming the limitations posed by the 
outdated infrastructure of the existing buildings.
