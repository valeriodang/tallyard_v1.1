Many countries have already recognised the economic, social and environmental  
benefits of prioritising building retrofit and reuse over demolition and rebuilding. Legislation proposed

The UK, however, has been slow to move on this issue, despite being home to some  
of the world’s leading architectural, engineering, construction, financial, legal,  Other non-legislative requirements
and insurance firms who are pioneers in green building development and delivery. No policy in place
The lack of UK central government policy clarity, financial incentives and sensible 
regulation supportive of a ‘Retrofit First’ approach leaves the UK vulnerable to not  
only missing its legal Paris Agreement commitments,9 but also to missing the  
enormous growth opportunity of retrofit and the circular economy, and to losing  
its position and reputation as a world leader in this space. 

Due to the lack of existing UK policy, regulation, and incentives for reuse, we decided 
to examine examples of best practice on a global scale, and we are confident we have 
sourced several excellent benchmarks for the UK to draw upon.

At Don’t Waste Buildings, we advocate for the delivery of UK housing and development 
through the reuse of existing buildings, and we expand on our thoughts in this paper. 

We are calling on the UK government to:

a) clarify planning policy on refurbishment/retrofit/reuse by implementing  
 ‘Retrofit First’ at a national level,
b)  implement sensible regulations, such as requiring whole-life carbon assessments 

(WLCA’s) for all demolition and rebuild schemes, to provide a level playing field  
for the property development industry, and

c)  introduce financial incentives to help make retrofit simpler and more profitable  
so that it, as opposed to wasteful demolition and new build, becomes the default 
approach for developers.

This paper focuses on the last of these three measures, financial incentives,  
as we believe it is the most meaningful area of reform.
