INTRODUCTION
WHY IS CIRCULAR ECONOMY 
IMPORTANT?
The construction sector is one of the largest consumers of 
materials and produces more waste than any other sector in 
the UK.1 In 2018, the UK produced 137.8 million tonnes of 
construction, demolition and excavation waste. Of this, 50% 
was construction and demolition waste, and 92% of this waste 
was recovered for onward use.1 However, much of the waste 
diverted from landfill is not reused or recycled, but rather is 
downgraded or incinerated. With population and consumption 
growth projected to increase, there will be increasing pressure 
on natural resources, and this will create resource scarcity and 
price volatility. Undoubtedly, offices contribute massively to 
this waste, forming 15% of the total commercial property sector 
and 44% of the construction sector’s total value.2 Alongside

RESEARCH AIM AND APPROACH
AIMS OF THIS REPORT
Considering the scale of the challenge identified in the previous 
section, this report aims to:

• raise general awareness about the opportunity for circularity
in office new-build, refurbishment and fit-out, and showcase
existing guidance

• provide an understanding of the key challenges in delivering
circular economy principles in new-build projects and the
fit-out of offices

• provide recommendations and solutions for stakeholders
along the value chain of the offices sector, with a focus on
fit-out, to support the shift to more sustainable and circular
approaches.

OUR APPROACH 80 Charlotte Street, London 
Courtesy of Arup. Copyright right  

This report builds on existing research and guidance available Make 2022
on the circular economy and the built environment. It identifies 
the challenges and opportunities related to embedding circular 
economy principles in the office fit-out sector, including 
reference to case studies. As part of this research, a literature 
review was undertaken, alongside interviews with experts 
within the office sector as well as circular economy experts. 
The final section of the report includes recommendations and 
actions that can be taken by industry players to support the 
transition to a circular economy. This research focuses on office 
fit-outs due to the increased frequency of short-term leases and 
the impact that Cat A fit-outs are having in relation to material 
extraction, waste generation, greenhouse gas emissions 
and species decline. However, much of the guidance and 
recommendations can, and should, be applied to office new-
build developments and refurbishments. ■

CURRENT APPROACH TO CIRCULAR ECONOMY 
IN THE OFFICE SECTOR
A range of sustainability certification standards 
exist to guide designers and contractors through 
a sustainable project, including the BREEAM 
standard specifically for fit-out.21 This standard 
categorises the elements of a fit-out and assigns 
credits based on compliance with best practice 
(e.g. appointing a sustainability champion, 
responsibly sourcing materials or conducting 
a pre-refurbishment audit). Categories such as 
Project Management and Setup, Materials, Waste 
and Innovation contain credits directly related to 
a circular fit-out. Other sustainability standards 
in the UK include the RICS SKA rating,22 which 
addresses fit-out, and the WELL Building Standard, 
which advocates health and wellbeing. In some Copyright wzfs1s | iStock
cases, office projects will be assessed against LEED 
and the Living Buildings Challenge, among others.
Currently, when addressing circular economy principles, 
projects will typically look to limit waste to landfill or to recycle 
waste where possible, but incorporating circular approaches 
around designing for deconstruction, integrating reuse 
materials and exploring issues such as designing for future 
scenarios and new business models is limited.

The next section identifies the challenges associated with 
shifting towards a new systems approach, where waste and 
pollution is no longer generated, material and products are 
recirculated, and nature is regenerated. ■

CONCLUSION
The transition away from a linear economy 
demands systems change. The circular economy 
creates value through engaging new business 
models and changing current operating systems. 
Keeping assets in circulation at their highest value 
is one of the key principles of a circular economy, 
and undoubtedly this will need to be addressed in 
the office sector.
The industry is undergoing rapid change due to both short- and 
long-term factors. Drivers for change, including the impact of 
the COVID-19 pandemic and the resulting changing attitudes to 
work, changes in the length of leases and a growing commitment 
to sustainability and net zero are all shaping the future of the 
office. This presents a big challenge for circularity as the industry
